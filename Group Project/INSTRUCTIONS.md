## Details

This assignment is designed to engage undergraduate students in researching and analyzing a real-world problem related to social networks. Students will investigate complex issues arising in social media platforms and develop both a written report and a presentation to communicate their findings and propose potential solutions.

Each group will identify, analyze, and propose solutions to a real-life problem associated with social networks. **Your project must incorporate and clearly apply several topics covered in class**, demonstrating a strong connection between theoretical concepts and real-world applications.

### Group Requirements

- This is a **group assignment**.
- Each group must consist of 6-7 **students**.
- All members are expected to contribute equally.
- Students must communicate regularly and in a timely manner with the group leader.
- Each group must select a specific problem related to social networks. Examples include (but are not limited to):
  - Spread of misinformation and fake news
  - Online harassment and hate speech
  - TikTok Trends and Information Cascades

### Use of Course Topics

Your analysis must explicitly integrate **multiple concepts from the course**, such as (but not limited to):

- Graph theory and network structure
- Centrality measures (degree, betweenness, clustering coefficient, etc.)
- Cascades and diffusion models
- Structural balance and signed networks
- Game theory and strategic interaction
- Market mechanisms and auctions
- Small-world phenomenon
- Epidemic models (SIR/SIS)
- Link analysis (PageRank, hubs/authorities)

You must clearly explain how these concepts help model, analyze, or solve the selected problem.

### Data and Analysis

Groups must identify and use relevant data (e.g., datasets) to analyze their chosen problem. After conducting the analysis, groups must propose practical and ethical solutions. Proposed solutions should:

- Be grounded in existing literature
- Be supported by the collected data
- Consider feasibility and potential real-world impact

### Written Report (4 Pages)

Each group must submit a formal report that includes:

- **Abstract**: A brief summary of the report.
- **Introduction**: Motivation and problem statement.
- **Notation / Model**: Description of the notation used and/or how the problem is modeled using course concepts.
- **Proposed Solution**: Explanation of the main techniques used to address the problem.
- **Conclusion and Future Work**: Your conclusions and possible future directions inspired by the project.

### Presentation (9 Slides Maximum)

Each group will deliver a presentation summarizing their findings and proposed solutions. Presentations should be clear, engaging, and well-organized. Visual aids such as charts, graphs, and images are encouraged.

Recommended slide structure:

- **Introduction (2 slides)**
  - Motivation
  - Problem statement
- **Notation / Model (1-2 slides)**
- **Proposed Solution (2-3 slides)**
- **Conclusion and Future Work (1-2 slides)**

### Assessment Criteria

Projects will be evaluated based on:

- Relevance and significance of the chosen problem
- Depth and correctness in applying multiple course topics
- Quality of the literature review
- Appropriateness of data collection and analysis
- Clarity and feasibility of proposed solutions
- Organization and writing quality of the report
- Effectiveness of the oral presentation

### Submission

The **group leader** is responsible for submitting:

- The final written report
- Delivering the presentation during the last week of classes
- A document clearly describing the contribution of each group member

All members must contribute meaningfully to the project.
